## Section 5: Brief

### Purpose and qualities (0501-0502)
A brief lets a senior officer take in a subject easily and quickly. Oral, written, or both. It must be: **as short as possible; accurate; draw attention to salient points; impartial**; and where necessary give constructive criticism with alternatives, and positive advice with the logic behind each recommendation.

### Types (0503)
- **Information brief**: informs a senior officer about a plan, situation or subject; for priority information, complex plans/systems/statistics/charts, or matters needing explanation. Framework below (Annex A).
- **Decision brief**: seeks a decision from an authority. Contains: statement of the problem; facts bearing on it; assumptions (if any); analysis; options; conclusion; recommendation(s). **Decision is sought at the end.** Framework below (Annex B).

### Oral brief (0504)
- Purpose: saves the senior officer reading himself in; lets him question the briefer and discuss with commanders/staff as it unfolds.
- From a 5-minute talk to a full-scale presentation; **always plan carefully**. PowerPoint often supports it (Annex C rules below).
- For an oral information brief, superscription and subscription may be dropped (5A note 3).
- Oral decision brief: seek the decision **after** the conclusion; written decision brief: **before** the conclusion (5B note 4).

### Written brief (0505-0508)
- Framework flexible (the senior officer's wishes or local practice), but follows service writing rules; **may be double spaced**.
- Use main, group and paragraph headings **liberally**; short crisp sentences.
- Use the heading **`Comment(s)`** wherever you give opinion rather than fact.
- No formal service-paper conclusion; end with a **short summary, with recommendation if appropriate**.
- Leave a **3 cm right-hand margin** for the reader's notes (flags are noted in it).
- Don't make the senior officer read whole documents: attach and **flag** them (0506).
- **Flag** = a complete attached document (or a major part/chapter of a very long one). **Side flag** = a specific passage inside a flagged document.
- Flag rules (0508):
  - All flags visible while reading, not sticking out enough to tear; must not hide each other or any writing.
  - Each is mentioned in the right-hand margin opposite the text: `A full statement is at ........ Flag A`.
  - Listed at the end of the brief under `List of Flags:` and `List of Side Flags:` so the brief can be re-assembled.
  - Every attached paper (document, letter, pamphlet, book, extract) gets a flag, lettered **A, B, C** in order, fixed at the top of its first page so they read left to right. Chapter starting on a reverse page: write `OVER` under the letter.
  - Side flags numbered **1, 2, 3** consecutively through the whole brief (irrespective of flag), fixed on the right edge opposite the paragraph; so they may appear out of order on a document.
  - Side-flagged paragraphs are side-lined in pencil on the outer edge. Passage on the reverse: `OVER` on the side flag; passage continuing over pages: `AND OVER`, continue the side-lining, and put a **kicker** at the top/bottom of each continuation page.
  - Side-flag list entry format: `1. Flag B, Sec XIX - Ty Trg Areas.`

### Checklist for a brief on a specific problem (0509)
a. How much history is needed, given what the recipient knows? b. Is a short summary of main points needed? c. Are the facts correct? d. Are assumptions clearly labelled? e. Any important point missed? f. Do the arguments conform to policy? g. Are the conclusions practicable? h. What to recommend (**no new material** in the recommendation)? j. What objections or counter-arguments may be raised?

### Personality brief (0510; example 5E)
Unless directed otherwise: a. admin details (accommodation, meals, arrival/departure time and method); b. questions to ask the visitor; c. questions the visitor may ask, with recommended answers; d. any opposing line he may take, with counter-argument; e. points to emphasise; f. arrangements for further meetings; g. short biography (past experience/appointments relevant to the subject, special interests or idiosyncrasies). Example headings: `Intro`, `Admin`, `Biography`; title `BRIEF FOR BASE CDR ON VISIT OF AIR CDRE A H CHOWDHURY`.

### Brief for visits (0511)
a. outline history of the establishment; b. current work/training; c. short biographies of hosts and key personalities (photos if available); d. higher organisation chart; e. questions the visitor might ask; f. questions hosts might ask, with recommended answers; g. special information (similar work elsewhere, security restrictions); h. timetable with all admin arrangements.

### Care of documents and multi-subject briefs (0512-0513)
- Note removals in the parent files; **classify the whole brief at least as high as the highest attached document**; return papers to their files afterwards; file the brief with its list of flags and file references.
- Several subjects (e.g. a meeting agenda): a separate brief per subject under a covering note.

### Annex A: Framework of an information brief
```
                SECURITY CLASSIFICATION
                                    Copy No .... of ....   (if SECRET or above)
                                    Total Pages ....       (if SECRET or above)
Identifying Reference
BRIEF FOR (APPT) ON (SUBJECT)
Introduction
1.   Background Info, if any.
2.   Purpose/Aim.
3.   Scope.
Body
4.   Background.
5.   Current/Existing State.
6.   Future Possibilities.
Conclusions
7.   Questions/Clarification.
8.   Concluding Statement.
                                    Signature / Signature Block / Date
                SECURITY CLASSIFICATION
```
Items may be added or dropped if the person briefed already knows the material.

### Annex B: Framework of a decision brief
```
Identifying Reference
BRIEF FOR (APPOINTMENT) ON (SUBJECT MATTER)
Ref(s):  A.  B.
Introduction
1.   Background Info if any.
2.   Purpose/Aim.
3.   Scope.
4.   To Specify that Decision is Solicited After/Before.
Body
5.   Arrange the main ideas in following sequences:
     a.  Statement of the problem.
     b.  Facts bearing on the problem.
     c.  Assumption(s) (if any).
     d.  Analyse the problem.
     e.  Options, advantage and disadvantages of each option.
     f.  Summary, if required.
     g.  Recommendation(s).
Conclusion
6.   Questions/Clarifications.
7.   Concluding Remarks.
8.   Seek Decision (for oral brief).
                                    Signature / Signature Block / Date
List of Flags:       A.  (Title of document)
List of Side Flags:  1.  (Flag, paragraphs or section number, title)
Distribution:                                      Copy No
```
Copy No / Total Pages / page `1 of 2` only if SECRET or above. Example (5D): CONFIDENTIAL `BRIEF FOR CGS ON USE OF TRG AREAS IN CHATTOGRAM HILL TRACTS (CHT)`, headings `Hist`, `Present Sit`, `Recom`, heavy abbreviation, flags in the right margin, `Info:` list only if copies are needed.

### Annex C: Guidelines for visual/PowerPoint presentation
1. **Slides** are an aid to the speaker, not a substitute; informative with principal words and headings; **no verbatim paragraphs or many lines**; an aide-memoire, not cluttered; concise yet comprehensive. The presenter, not the slides, must hold the audience.
2. **Security classification**: routine presentations below CONFIDENTIAL may show none. If the presentation **or any slide** merits **SECRET or higher**, put the classification **in the middle, block capitals, unabbreviated, not underlined, in a distinct colour, on every slide** (even if only one slide needs it). Presenter is responsible for the security of the file on the devices used.
3. **Staff-duties deviations allowed** (otherwise follow service writing rules):
   a. headings need not be underlined;
   b. paragraph/sub-paragraph text may be bulleted;
   c. no full stop after a bulleted heading that stands alone; full stop when a paragraph/sub-paragraph has a heading followed by text;
   d. **slide number bottom right** (or as appropriate);
   e. designed formats/shapes may deviate from staff duties;
   f. keep it simple: essential points only; **avoid transitions and animation** unless necessary.
4. **Other rules**:
   a. font size **24-44**; colours **black, white, yellow**;
   b. size depends on screen, room and audience;
   c. contrast and clarity suited to conditions and audience, soothing to the eye;
   d. background **white or navy blue**;
   e. other colours/fonts only for special requirements;
   f. cite sources/credit others' work;
   g. not text-only: use **graphs, charts, diagrams** as far as feasible;
   h. design must not distract from the key message;
   j. **rehearse to stay within time**;
   k. have a **reading script** for formal presentations; people speak **100-120 words per minute**, so script length = minutes × ~110 words.

## Section 8: Graphical presentation (0801-0804)

- Graphs/charts must serve a purpose, not decorate: show statistical significance at a glance; simplify masses of numbers; show past events or forecast trends; plan and present programmes of interrelated events.
- Plotting: X axis = selected/predetermined variable, Y axis = observed/calculated variable; each axis labelled with quantity and units; multiple scales on one axis must be unambiguous; scale as large as space allows, chosen for easy accurate reading and **never distorted by stretching one axis**.
- **Simplicity**: as self-explanatory as possible, no overcrowding, brief notes; split into 2+ diagrams if too complex.
- **Display**: vivid but simple; **normally no more than 5** diagrams; for reproduction use reproducible materials (colours may need to become shading).
- **Layout**: subject heading (e.g. `Figure 1: Statistics of Course Participants in DSCSC`), **source bottom left** (`Source: Armed Forces Division`), basis of calculations, legend/key if needed. See also para 0284 (figure numbering).
